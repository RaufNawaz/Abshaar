# Original

وَاہ وَا رمزِ سجَن دِی

واہ وا، رمزِ سجن دی ہور!

کوٹھے تے چڑھ دیواں ہوکا
عشق وہاجو کوئی نہ لوکا
اس دا مُول نہ کھانا دھوکا
جنگل، بستی مِلے نہ مذکور

شے دیدار ہویا بد راہی
اَچن چیت پَئی گل پَچھاہی
ڈاہڈی کیتی بے پرواہی
مَینوں مِلیا ٹھگ لاہور
واہ وا، رمزِ سجن دی ہور!

عاشق پھر دے چُپ چُپاتے
جِیہے مست سدا، مدھ ماتے
دامِ زُلف دے اَندر پھاتے
اوتھے چلے وَس نہ زور

بُلّھیا! شوہ دا نِیوں انوکھا
جو دیکھے سو کے نہ لیکھے
اُس دا رنگ، نہ رُوپ، نہ ریکھ اے
اوہی ہووے ہو کے چور
واہ وا، رمزِ سجن دی ہور!

# Transliteration

wah wa, ramz-i sajjan di hor!

koṭhe te chaṛh diwaan hoka
ishq wahaaju koi nah loka
is da mool nah khaana dhoka
jangal, basti mile nah mazkoor

shai deedaar hoya bad-raahi
achan chet pai gal pachhaahi
ḍaahḍi keeti be-parwaahi
mainun miliya ṭhag lahore
wah wa, ramz-i sajjan di hor!

aashiq phir de chup chupaate
jeehe mast sada, madh maate
daam-i zulf de andar phaate
othe chale was nah zor

bullhiya! shoh da niyoon anokha
jo dekhe so ke nah lekhe
us da rang, nah roop, nah rekh e
ohi howe ho ke chor
wah wa, ramz-i sajjan di hor!

# Literal Translation

Strange are the ways of my love!

To sleep I have kissed goodbye;
on the roof I mope and sigh
while mirages green my eye.
No peace in city or grove.

To glimpse is to die. One look
and the noose is round your neck.
I should have kept it in check,
but Lahore's thug covered his bluff.

Strange are the ways of my love!

Lovers walk silently where
is nobody else, and they stare
through the bars of silken hair,
strength from their limbs drained off.

Bulleh, what can you hope to find
when love is totally blind?
It is just a state of mind
that knows nor smooth nor rough.

Strange are the ways of my love!

_Reference translation by Taufiq Rafat, "Bulleh Shah: A Selection" (Vanguard, 1982), pp.173, 175. COPYRIGHTED — private reference only; do NOT publish or use for training._

# AI Translation

How wonderful — the mystery of the Beloved is something else!

I climb onto the rooftop and call out:
is there anyone who deals in love?
Its price is nothing less than swallowing deceit,
yet neither in the wild nor in the town is it even spoken of.

The instant of the vision led me astray;
all at once the matter fell upon me from behind.
It played a fierce indifference with me —
the thug of Lahore is the one I met.
How wonderful — the mystery of the Beloved is something else!

The lovers move about hushed and silent,
as if forever drunk, intoxicated with wine,
caught fast in the snare of the beloved's tresses —
there no strength or force will avail.

Bulleh! the Beloved's love is a wondrous thing:
whoever has seen it does not count it up.
It has no colour, no form, no outline —
he alone becomes it who first becomes its thief.
How wonderful — the mystery of the Beloved is something else!
