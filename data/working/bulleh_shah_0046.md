# Original

مائی قدم کریندی یار

مائی جوڑا، مائی گھوڑا، مائی دا اسوار
مائی نوں مائی دوڑائے، مائی دا کھڑکار
مائی قدم کریندی یار!

مائی نوں مائی مارن لگی، مائی دا ہتھیار
جس مائی پر بُہتی مائی، تِس مائی ہنکار
مائی قدم کریندی یار!

مائی باغ، بغیچہ مائی، مائی دی گلزار
مائی نوں مائی دیکھن آئی، مائی دی اے بہار
مائی قدم کریندی یار!

چار رِیاں رَل کھیڈن لگیاں، پنجویں وِچ سردار
ہس کھیڈ مُڑ مائی ہویاں، پوندیاں پَیر پسار
مائی قدم کریندی یار!

# Transliteration

māī qadam karendī yār

māī joṛā, māī ghoṛā, māī dā asvār
māī nūṁ māī doṛāe, māī dā khaṛkār
māī qadam karendī yār!

māī nūṁ māī māran lagī, māī dā hathiyār
jis māī par buhtī māī, tis māī hankār
māī qadam karendī yār!

māī bāgh, baghīchā māī, māī dī gulzār
māī nūṁ māī dekhan āī, māī dī e bahār
māī qadam karendī yār!

chār riyāṁ ral kheḍan lagiyāṁ, panjveṁ vich sardār
has kheḍ muṛ māī hoyāṁ, poṁdiyāṁ pair pasār
māī qadam karendī yār!

# Literal Translation

The horse is clay, and so is the rider —
how strangely clay runs after clay,

	that's its way.

And clay kills clay with a clay sword;
the more clayey one will be the one to slay,

	that's its way.

What's the garden but clay? And its flowers?
Clay has come to it in a season of clay,

	that's its way.

Four elements spin the world, but a fifth
overrules them, while the rest betray;

	that's their way.

_Reference translation by Taufiq Rafat, "Bulleh Shah: A Selection" (Vanguard, 1982), p.151. COPYRIGHTED — private reference only; do NOT publish or use for training._

# AI Translation

The clay takes the pace of the beloved.

Clay the saddle, clay the horse, clay the rider,
clay makes clay gallop, clay is the clatter of it.
The clay takes the pace of the beloved!

Clay set out to slay clay, clay the weapon;
whichever clay has the more clay, that clay is proud.
The clay takes the pace of the beloved!

Clay the garden, clay the orchard, clay its rose-bed;
clay came to gaze on clay, clay is the springtime of it.
The clay takes the pace of the beloved!

Four elements joined together to play,
and in the fifth is the chieftain;
having laughed and played, they turn back to clay,
falling and stretching out their feet.
The clay takes the pace of the beloved!
