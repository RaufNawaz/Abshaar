# Original

سب اِکو رنگ کپاہیں دا

سب اِکو رنگ کپاہیں دا
تانی، تانا، پیٹا، نلیاں
پیٹھ، نڑا، تے چھبا، چھلیاں
آپو اپنے نام جتاون
وکھو وکھی جائیں دا
سب اِکو رنگ کپاہیں دا

چُنی، پینی، کھدر، دھوتر
مَلمل، خاصہ، اِکّا سُوتر
پُوئی وِچوں باہر آوے
بھگوا بھیس گُسائیں دا
سب اِکو رنگ کپاہیں دا

گُڑیاں، ہتّھیں، چھاپاں، چھلّے
آپو اپنے نام سَئُلے
سبھا اِکّا چاندی آکھو
کنگن، چُھڑا باہیں دا
سب اِکو رنگ کپاہیں دا

# Literal Translation

All cotton-bolls are white.

Warp and woof, spindle and reel,
back and front, head and heel,
will end up in the one cloth
that will not last the night.

    All cotton-bolls are white.

Khaddar and muslin, coarse or fine,
dress for ploughman, groom, divine,
issue from the self-same loom
no matter what the guise.

    All cotton-bolls are white.

The bangles worn by girls, and rings
and bracelets and such adornings
are from the same silver beaten,
though hands are dark or light.

All cotton-bolls are white.

_Reference translation by Taufiq Rafat, "Bulleh Shah: A Selection" (Vanguard, 1982), p.107. COPYRIGHTED — private reference only; do NOT publish or use for training. Rafat compresses the long Punjabi catalogues of loom-parts and cloths into representative pairs._

# AI Translation

All are of one colour — all of them cotton.

Warp-thread and warp-beam, weft and spools,
the back-beam, the reed, the shuttle, the rings —
each announces a name of its own,
each goes about under a separate name;
all are of one colour — all of them cotton.

Chunni and fine weave, khaddar and dhoti-cloth,
muslin and khaasa — all a single thread;
each comes forth from the very same spool,
even the saffron robe of the ascetic.
All are of one colour — all of them cotton.

Bangles, arm-rings, signet-rings and bands,
each of them with a name of its own —
call it all one and the same silver:
the kangan, the churra on the arms —
all are of one colour — all of them cotton.
