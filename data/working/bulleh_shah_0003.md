اک نقطے دا فرق ہے: بھلی پھرے گوئیں

# Transliteration

Jaisi sūrat ʿain di, waisi sūrat ġain

# Reference Translation

O and Q are much alike, except for a squiggle;
to think one tiny stroke has made the whole world wriggle.

# Tashreeh

This couplet is the compressed twin of the alphabet kafi (see 0002): ain (ع) and ghain (غ) are the same glyph distinguished only by a single dot (nuqta), and Bulleh Shah makes that dot the emblem of the whole problem of appearance and reality. The analytic move is to treat orthography as ontology — the "difference" the world takes to be decisive is graphically trivial, yet "over that one dot the bewildered world keeps wandering." The implication is a critique of how human distinctions — of doctrine, sect, caste, ritual, name — are erected on differences that, seen from the vantage of the One, are as slight as a dot, and yet consume people in dispute and aimless wandering. The bewilderment named here is the negative ḥayra of the unrealized (disorientation), not the ecstatic perplexity the gnostic prizes.

The form carries the argument. Like a mathnawi aphorism the couplet states a likeness (the two letters' shared shape) and then a hairline difference, enacting in miniature the tension between waḥda, unity of form, and kathra, multiplicity of appearance; the poem is thus a two-line performance of the doctrine the longer alphabet kafi expounds. A textual caution for review: the last word (read tentatively as ku'in, "wandering") and the opening word (bhalli, "good/simple," or possibly jhalli, "the mad one") are flagged uncertain, and since the reading of the second line turns on them, this interpretation should be treated as provisional.

# Key Terms

- nuqta: the single dot distinguishing ain from ghain — the minimal, near-illusory difference on which the world's disputes turn; do_not_flatten_to a punctuation mark.
- ain / ghain: two letters identical but for the dot, used as an emblem of outward difference over inner sameness; do_not_flatten_to mere letters.
- ḥayra: bewildered wandering — here the disorientation of those who mistake the dot for the substance; do_not_flatten_to ordinary confusion.
