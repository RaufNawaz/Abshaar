# Original

کدی آ مِل یار پیاریا

دُور و دُور اساتھوں گیئوں
اصلاتے جا کے بہ رہیئوں
کیوں قصور وِساریا
کدی آ مِل یار پیاریا

در کھُلّا حَشر عذاب دا
بُرا حال ہویا پنجاب دا
وچ ہاویلے دوزخ ساڑیا
کدی آ مِل یار پیاریا

کدیں آویں وَڈھ پرواریا
تیرے دُکھاں سانوں ماریا
مُنہ بارھویں صدی پلساریا
کدی آ مِل یار پیاریا

بُلھا! شوہ میرے گھر آوسی
میری بلدی بھاہ بُجھاوسی
عنایت دَم دَم نال چتاریا
کدی آ مِل یار پیاریا

# Script Notes

- Script: Shahmukhi (Perso-Arabic)
- Language spans: Punjabi
- Notes: Vision transcription of Rafat poem 38, read from the calligraphic Nastaliq facing page (book p.128). Refrain "Kadi aa mil yaar pyaariya" (come meet me sometime, dear friend). A rare topical/historical kafi: it laments the ruin of Punjab and names the "twelfth century" (Hijri, = 18th c. CE) and the murshid Inayat. Line 3 puns Qasoor (Bulleh's town Kasur) / qasoor (fault). Several interior lines are hard calligraphy — flagged. Needs native/scholarly verification.

# Literal Translation

Come see me once in a while.

You are gone, friend, far away
beyond my reach. Many a day
Kasur has missed your smile.
    Come see me once in while.

Now hell has opened its bogs.
Punjab has gone to the dogs.
This hell is the nethermost hell.
    Come see me once in a while.

A brief visit now and then
will give some ease to my pain.
This century cramps our style.
    Come see me once in a while.

The flames engulfing my house,
Bulleh, only love can douse.
Inayat calls up the aisle
    Come see me once in a while.

_Reference translation by Taufiq Rafat, "Bulleh Shah: A Selection" (Vanguard, 1982), p.129. COPYRIGHTED — private reference only; do NOT publish or use for training. Rafat keeps "Kasur" and "Inayat" but generalises "the twelfth century" to "This century cramps our style."_

# AI Translation

Come, meet me sometime, beloved friend.

You have gone far, far away from us,
and settled somewhere beyond my reach;
why have you forgotten Kasur?
Come, meet me sometime, beloved friend.

The door of doomsday-torment has swung open,
Punjab has fallen into a wretched state;
within these halls a hell now burns.
Come, meet me sometime, beloved friend.

Come to me at least sometime, O kinsman —
the griefs of your absence have slain me;
the twelfth century has bared its ugly face.
Come, meet me sometime, beloved friend.

Bulleh — the Beloved will surely come to my house
and quench the fire that blazes within me.
With every breath I call Inayat to mind.
Come, meet me sometime, beloved friend.
