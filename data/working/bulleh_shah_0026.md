# Original

خَلق تماشے آئی یار

خَلق تماشے آئی یار!
اَج کیہ کیتا؛ کل کیہ کرنا؛ وَت اساڈا آیا!
اَیسی واہ کِیاری بیجی، چڑیاں کھیت وَنجایا

اِک اُلانبھا سِیاں دا ہے، دُوجا ہے سنسار
ننگ ناموس اِتھوں دے ایتھے، لاہ پکڑی بھوئیں یار

نِڈھا کردا، بُڈھا کردا، آپو اپنی واری
کیہ بی بی، کیہ باندی لَونڈی، کیہ دھوبن بھٹیاری

بُلھا شوہ نوں دیکھن جاوے، آپ بہانہ کر دا
گوں گوئی بھانڈے گھڑ کے، ٹھیکریاں کر دھر دا

ایہ تماشا دیکھ کے چل پَو، اگلا دیکھ بازار
واہ وا پُھنج پَئی دربار، خَلق تماشے آئی یار!

# Transliteration

Khalq tamaashe aai yaar

Khalq tamaashe aai yaar!
Aj keh keeta; kal keh karna; vat asaaḍa aaya!
Aisi vaah kiaari beeji, chiṛiyaan khet vanjaaya

Ik ulaanbha siyaan da hai, dooja hai sansaar
Nang naamoos ithon de aithe, laah pakṛi bhoin yaar

Niḍha karda, buḍha karda, aapo apni vaari
Keh bibi, keh baandi launḍi, keh dhoban bhaṭiaari

Bulha shoh nu dekhan jaave, aap bahaana karda
Gaun-goi bhaanḍe ghaṛ ke, ṭheekriyaan kar dhar da

Eh tamaasha dekh ke chal pau, agla dekh bazaar
Vaah va phunj pai darbaar, khalq tamaashe aai yaar!

# Literal Translation

The world's a fun-fair.

What did I do today? What of tomorrow?
A bad ploughing will only bring sorrow.

The world and my love made me their slave.
But you'll go empty-handed to the grave.

Each in his turn youngster and old man fade,
innkeeper and servingman, mistress and maid.

To win his love, Bulleh exercises his wits;
first fashions a pot, then shatters it to bits.

Stroll through the bazar, join the revelry there,
then on to the next. The world's a fun-fair.

_Reference translation by Taufiq Rafat, "Bulleh Shah: A Selection" (Vanguard, 1982), p.95. COPYRIGHTED — private reference only; do NOT publish or use for training. Rafat renders the refrain "khalq tamaashe aai" as "The world's a fun-fair" and re-orders/compresses the imagery._

# AI Translation

The people have come out to the spectacle, O friend!

What did I do today? What must I do tomorrow? — and now our turn has come round!
Such was the plot I sowed that the birds laid the whole field to waste.

One reproach comes from the loved ones, the other from the world;
the honour and shame of this place stay here — one lets go and clutches at the dust, O friend.

He makes one young, He makes one old — each gets his own turn:
be it noblewoman or bondmaid or slave-girl, be it washerwoman or innkeeper's wife.

Bulleh sets out to see the Beloved, and makes of himself a pretext;
having shaped all manner of pots, He breaks them back into shards.

Watch this show and pass along; go and look at the next bazaar;
what a throng has gathered at the court — the people have come out to the spectacle, O friend!
