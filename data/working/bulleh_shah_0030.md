# Transliteration

Dharamsaal dhaaṛvi vasde, ṭhaakur duaare ṭhag

# Literal Translation

Merchants in mosque and monastery, thugs in the landlord's pay.
The lover alone goes his secret, inviolable way.

# Tashreeh

This epigrammatic couplet delivers Bulleh Shah's anti-institutional critique with pointed even-handedness: the holy places of both religions are indicted together. The dharamsāl (the temple rest-house / pilgrim lodge) shelters "robbers" (dhāṛvī, dacoits), the ṭhākur-duārā (the gate of the Hindu idol-temple) harbours "cheats" (ṭhag), and — the couplet insists — "inside the mosque too" the same frauds have taken their seats. By naming Hindu and Muslim sacred institutions in one breath and finding both equally overrun by hypocrites, Bulleh Shah refuses to spare his own tradition; this is not sectarian point-scoring but a flat verdict on organized religion as such, which has become a refuge for the dishonest rather than the honest.

Against this stands the single figure who holds himself apart: "only the lover (āshiq) keeps separate." The lover's "way" is precisely not to be found in the institutions — neither temple nor mosque — because the true relation to the Real is interior and cannot be housed in establishments the ṭhag has colonized. This is the recurrent Bulleh Shah placement of ʿishq outside and above institutional religion (cf. 0013, 0031): the lover is the one who has left the marketplace of holy places for the direct, unhoused path. The couplet's compression — two lines that condemn three institutions and exempt one figure — is itself the point; the verdict needs no elaboration. (Line 2's first half is flagged HIGH uncertainty, so its exact wording is provisional, though the sense — cheats in the mosque too, the lover apart — is secure.)

# Key Terms

- dharamsāl: the temple rest-house / pilgrim lodge — a Hindu institution here sheltering robbers; do_not_flatten_to a generic shelter.
- ṭhākur-duārā: the gate of the Hindu idol-temple — harbouring cheats; do_not_flatten_to a neutral doorway.
- ṭhag: cheat, fraud, "thug" — the type who has colonized the holy places; do_not_flatten_to a petty thief.
- maseet: the mosque — named alongside the Hindu sites as equally overrun, so the critique spares no tradition; do_not_flatten_to a mere building.
- āshiq: the lover — the one who keeps apart from all institutions on love's direct path; do_not_flatten_to a romantic.
