# Reference Translation

What I've picked up costs me dear.

Its thorns are sharp, my clothes are torn,
I must deal with one cold to the bone,
his servants tax each stick I own
which is quite a bundle.  As soon
as it is tied, the merchants will be here.

What I've picked up costs me dear.

The slope is steep, and heavy the load
on my head, and I stumble on the road.
My misspent life tells on my face,
I am so weak and lacking in grace,
not Bulleh, but Inayat must steer.

What I've picked up costs me dear.

_Reference translation by Taufiq Rafat, "Bulleh Shah: A Selection" (Vanguard, 1982), p.169. COPYRIGHTED — private reference only; do NOT publish or use for training._

# Tashreeh

The poem builds its allegory of a wasted life from the labour of a woman picking kasumbha (safflower) — or, in Rafat's reading, gathering a taxed load of firewood — under a rapacious estate hierarchy. The controlling image is fruitless toil: she gathers the blossoms "one by one," torn by thorns, only to have "the merchants carry off" everything she heaps up, while a harsh overseer, a tyrant record-keeper (paṭwārī), and "four headmen" extract a "heavy levy" at every turn. Read as the life of the soul in the world, this is the futility of worldly labour — a lifetime of painful gathering whose harvest is seized by others (death, the world, the appetites), leaving the labourer nothing. The refrain's blunt verdict, "one by one I picked — and lost," names the outcome: all that effort, and the game is lost.

The second stanza shifts from the field to the road home, and the allegory turns explicitly eschatological: "the pass (ghāṭī) is steep, the road hard, and the bundle (gaṭhṛī) on my head heavy." The mountain pass is the passage out of the world — death and the reckoning — and the heavy bundle is the load of deeds and sins the soul must carry over it. The stark line "the doers of good deeds (amlāṅ wāliyāṅ) have all crossed over; only I, the sinner (ogaṇhārī), am left behind" places the speaker among the failures, whose deeds were not enough to get them across, and "I have thrown my whole life away at play (kheḍ) and lost the game (bāzī) at the final throw" makes the wasted life a gamble comprehensively lost.

The closing is pure self-abasement resolving into dependence on the murshid — the recurrent Bulleh Shah move. She heaps disqualifications on herself — "low-born, loud-mouthed, ill-favoured, without merit, a wretched thing" — and then the humbling admission that gives the poem its point: "Bulleh is not worthy of the Beloved (shoh de lāyaq nāhīṅ); Shah Inayat, ferry me across (tārī)." Precisely because her own labour has failed and her merit is nil, the soul cannot cross by its own works; the only hope is the murshid, Shah Inayat, who ferries the unworthy across the hard pass. The theology matches the spinning poems (0036, 0040) and "Enough of learning" (0035) — one's own accumulation is worthless or seized, and salvation is granted by the master's grace, not earned — but here it is voiced from the far side of failure, by a soul that has lost the game and thrown itself on the ferryman. (The opening word kasumbha/load and several interior readings are flagged moderate uncertainty; the refrain and the Shah Inayat close are clear.)

# Key Terms

- kasumbha: safflower blossoms picked one by one — fruitless worldly labour whose harvest is seized by others (Rafat reads the image as a taxed load of firewood); do_not_flatten_to a botanical note.
- paṭwārī / muqaddam: the record-keeper and headmen who extract levies — the rapacious officialdom figuring the world's exactions on the soul; do_not_flatten_to neutral officials.
- ghāṭī / gaṭhṛī: the steep pass and the heavy bundle — death's passage and the load of deeds carried over it; do_not_flatten_to literal terrain and luggage.
- ogaṇhārī: the sinner / worthless woman left behind while the virtuous cross — the speaker's self-placement among the failed; do_not_flatten_to a mild self-criticism.
- Shah Inayat (tārī): the murshid who "ferries across" the soul too unworthy to cross by its own works; do_not_flatten_to a helper.
