# Original

مُنہ آئی بات نہ رہندی اے

سچ کہواں تے بھانبڑ مچدا اے
جھوٹھ آکھیاں کُجھ نہ بچدا اے
جی دوہاں گلاں توں جچدا اے
سچ سچ کے جیہا کہندی اے
مُنہ آئی بات نہ رہندی اے

ایہہ تلکن بازی وِہڑا اے
تم تم کے تُرو اندھیرا اے
وَر اندر دیکھو کیہڑا اے
کیوں خلقت باہر ڈھونڈ دی نیندی اے
مُنہ آئی بات نہ رہندی اے

جِس پایا بھیت قلندر دا
راہ کھوجیا اپنے اندر دا
اوہ واسی ہے سُکھ مندر دا
جِتّھے کوئی نہ چڑھدی لہندی اے
مُنہ آئی بات نہ رہندی اے

اِک لازم شرطِ اُدب دی اے
سانوں بات معلومی سب دی اے
ہر ہر وِچ صُورت رب دی اے
کِتّے ظاہر، کِتّے چھپیندی اے
مُنہ آئی بات نہ رہندی اے

اَساں پڑھیا عِلم تحقیقی اے
اوتھے اِکو حَرف حقیقی اے
ہور جھگڑا سب ودھیکی اے
ایویں رَولا پا پا بہندی اے
مُنہ آئی بات نہ رہندی اے

شوہ بُلھا! ساں تِیں دُکھ نہیں
بِن شوہ دے دُوجا سُکھ نہیں
پر دیکھن والی اَکھ نہیں
تہِیں جان جُدائیاں سہندی اے
مُنہ آئی بات نہ رہندی اے

# Reference Translation

Truth will out

        though it adds fuel to fire.
        Lies are a burnt-out pyre.
        So after looking at both
                the tongue must shout,
                truth will out.

        In the darkness walk with care
        for floors are slippery here.
        Ascertain who is within
                while others hunt without.
                Truth will out.

        Who knows what the sadhu knows
        will surely find repose.
        On the tideless river of peace
                he is afloat.
                Truth will out.

        Whatever the time and place
        God is in every face.
        Unalterable this fact
                covert or not.
                Truth will out.

        The lessons experience taught
        have all been dearly bought.
        The rest is a stream's babble
                not worth a thought.
                Truth will out.

        Love, Bulleh, is part of us
        without which is nothing but fuss.
        If only we had the eyes
                we would not doubt.
                Truth will out.

_Reference translation by Taufiq Rafat, "Bulleh Shah: A Selection" (Vanguard, 1982), p.159–161. COPYRIGHTED — private reference only; do NOT publish or use for training._

# AI Translation

The word that rises to the lips will not stay unspoken

If I speak the truth, a blaze flares up;
if I tell a lie, nothing is saved.
The heart weighs both these matters —
truth speaks out just as truth is.
The word that rises to the lips will not stay unspoken.

This is a slippery playing-field, a courtyard;
tread step by step, for it is dark.
Look which one is the bridegroom within —
why does creation go searching outside?
The word that rises to the lips will not stay unspoken.

Whoever has grasped the secret of the qalandar,
who has sought the path within himself,
he is a dweller in the temple of bliss,
a place where none rises and none sets.
The word that rises to the lips will not stay unspoken.

One condition is binding — the courtesy of restraint;
to us the matter of all is known.
In each and every one is the face of the Lord,
somewhere manifest, somewhere hidden.
The word that rises to the lips will not stay unspoken.

We have studied the science of inquiry —
there is but one true letter there;
all the rest of the wrangling is excess,
people just sit raising a needless din.
The word that rises to the lips will not stay unspoken.

O my Beloved, Bulleh! Away from you there is no grief that is grief;
without the Beloved there is no other joy.
But there is no eye that truly sees,
and so the soul endures its separations.
The word that rises to the lips will not stay unspoken.

# Literary Translation

The word once risen to the lips will not stay unsaid.

Speak the truth, and a blaze leaps up;
tell a lie, and nothing is spared.
The heart weighs the two and knows —
truth speaks the truth as it is.
The word will not stay unsaid.

This courtyard is a slippery floor;
tread softly, for the dark is deep.
Look within — who is the bridegroom there?
Why does the world go groping outside?
The word will not stay unsaid.

Whoever has caught the qalandar's secret
and traced the road within himself
dwells in the temple of peace,
where nothing rises and nothing sets.
The word will not stay unsaid.

One rule is binding — the courtesy of restraint;
and yet the matter of all is known to us.
In every face is the face of the Lord,
here made plain, there hidden away.
The word will not stay unsaid.

We studied the science of inquiry:
there is one true letter, and one alone;
all the rest of the quarrel is surplus,
a din that people raise and sit in.
The word will not stay unsaid.

Beloved — Bulleh! — no grief is grief but your absence;
without you there is no other joy.
But the eye that could see is not there,
and so the soul goes on bearing its partings.
The word once risen to the lips will not stay unsaid.

# Tashreeh

The kafi is built as an argument, and its refrain — munh āī bāt nah rahndī, "the word that has risen to the lips will not stay unspoken" — works as both premise and returning conclusion, recurring after each stanza the way a proof comes back to its thesis. On the surface it is a Punjabi proverb about the impossibility of holding one's tongue; beneath it is an epistemological claim about ḥaqīqa, realized truth: what has genuinely been seen cannot be un-said. The opening stanza at once prices that claim — "speak the truth and a blaze flares up; tell a lie and nothing is saved" — stranding the seeker between two fires and refusing the safety of silence. Historically this is the memory of the shaṭaḥāt, the "ecstatic utterances" that cost Sufis their lives: Hallaj crucified for "Anā'l-Ḥaqq," Sarmad beheaded in Mughal Delhi. The tension the poem deliberately stages is the one the tradition never resolved — the compulsion of utterance against the discipline it names two stanzas later, "the courtesy of restraint" (adab) and the guarding of the secret (kitmān al-sirr). Bulleh Shah does not choose between them; he dramatizes their collision.

The middle stanzas trace one movement, outside to inside. The world is "a slippery courtyard," a place of moral and epistemic hazard where one must "tread softly, for the dark is deep," and the decisive question is turned inward: "who is the bridegroom within? why does creation grope outside?" The nuptial image is doing analytic work — the Beloved sought in shrine, ritual, and pilgrimage is in fact the indwelling groom, so the whole apparatus of externalized religion is mislocated desire. The one who "catches the qalandar's secret" — the antinomian dervish who has broken with respectable form — "dwells in the temple of peace, where nothing rises and nothing sets." That phrase is the poem's metaphysical center: a state beyond chaṛhdī/lahndī, rising and setting, i.e. beyond the play of opposites and the flux of time, the still point of tawḥīd where duality — and with it anxiety, comparison, striving — dissolves.

From this vantage the poem states its boldest doctrine flatly: "in every face is the face of the Lord … here made plain, there hidden away." Analytically this is waḥdat al-wujūd, Ibn ʿArabi's "unity of being," in which the cosmos is a theatre of tajalliyāt, the ceaseless self-disclosures of the one Real, each face a mirror in which the Absolute sees and is seen. Its ethical implication — the one worth pressing — is immediate and radical: if God is present in every human being, then to love any human is to love God and to despise any human is to despise Him, a socially explosive claim in caste- and creed-bound eighteenth-century Punjab, of a piece with Bulleh Shah's lifelong assault on the qazi's and mullah's boundary-drawing. The same intuition is condensed in the much-recited Persian line har lahza ba shakl-e but-e ʿayyār bar-āmad — "at every moment the Beloved came forth in the guise of a new idol" — where one actor moves behind an endless costume-change of forms; Kabir reaches the same place from the nirguṇa side, refusing every idol precisely because the divine is the formless indweller of all. It is worth naming the fault line as well: cautious theologians read such lines through waḥdat al-shuhūd, "unity of witnessing" — God beheld everywhere but not identical with the seen — to protect transcendence, whereas Bulleh Shah's language leans toward the wujūdī pole, part of what made him dangerous. (One caution for the record: the Persian verse is a waḥdat commonplace of contested attribution, not securely Kabir's, and should be attributed carefully before any published citation.)

The fourth movement turns the argument against learning itself: "we studied the science of inquiry — there is one true letter, and one alone." The "true letter" is the Alif — first letter, a single unbroken vertical stroke, the numeral one — and thus the natural cipher for the undivided Source from which every other letter, every multiplicity, is drawn. This is the ḥurūfī/abjad strand of the tradition and Bulleh Shah's signature move, the same reduction he performs in "Alif Allah," where to master Alif is to have learned everything and the rest is "din," noise. The point is epistemological: he ranks maʿrifa, direct tasted knowing, above ʿilm, acquired discursive learning, and dismisses scholastic disputation not because it is false but because it is surplus — motion mistaking itself for arrival.

The closing stanza executes the sharpest reversal. Having insisted that the Beloved is in every face, Bulleh Shah confesses that the grief is undiminished: "no grief is grief but your absence … but the eye that could see is not there." The logic is exact and devastating — the problem was never the Beloved's absence, since He is everywhere present, but the absence of the organ of sight. The veil lies in the seer, not the seen. Only fanāʾ, the annihilation of the self-regarding ego, opens the "eye of the heart" (baṣīra) that perceives what is already, everywhere, the case; until it opens, the soul "goes on bearing its separations." The refrain now lands differently than at the start: the word that will not stay unspoken is finally this — that union is the fact and separation only a failure of vision, a truth the tongue is compelled to utter even while the eye cannot yet confirm it.

# Key Terms

- munh āī bāt: the word that has risen to the lips and so cannot be withheld — irrepressible realized truth; do_not_flatten_to idle talk.
- qalandar: the antinomian wandering dervish, here the holder of the hidden secret; do_not_flatten_to a mere beggar or eccentric.
- haraf haqīqī: the one "true letter" (the Alif, the single Reality) set against scholastic learning; do_not_flatten_to a literal letter of the alphabet.
- Shoh: the Beloved — a Punjabi endearment for God, the divine spouse; do_not_flatten_to a human lover only.
- ilm tahqīqī: the science of rational inquiry, here critiqued as surplus wrangling; do_not_flatten_to praiseworthy scholarship.
