# Draft email to Sufinama / Rekhta Foundation

**To:** contact@sufinama.org
**Cc:** contact@rekhta.org
**Subject:** Collaboration request — an open-source, academic archive of Sufi poetry (beginning with Bulleh Shah)

---

Dear Sufinama and Rekhta Foundation team,

My name is Rauf Nawaz, and I am an undergraduate student at Harvard College. I am writing to introduce a project I am building and to ask whether you would be open to working together on it.

Sufinama has been one of the most valuable resources I have found for this poetry — the care you have put into gathering Sufi and Sant kalaam and making it readable across Urdu, Hindi, and Roman script is remarkable, and it is a large part of why the tradition is reachable for a new generation. My project grows directly out of that same purpose.

The project (working name **Abshaar**) is a free, open-source, non-commercial, academic archive and interpretation system for Punjabi, Urdu, and Persian-influenced Sufi poetry. It is deliberately *not* a one-click translator. For each poem it keeps every layer separate and visible — the original text and script, a transliteration, a close literal gloss, a literary translation, a *tashreeh* (explanation of metaphor and context), a glossary of key terms, poet context, and full source citations — so that a reader can always see what is documented fact and what is interpretation. Every interpretive layer is human-reviewed. The goal is to carry this poetry to English-speaking and global readers *without flattening* its cultural and metaphysical meaning.

I am beginning with Bulleh Shah. Because your Terms of Service ask for prior written authorization before any Sufinama content is reused, I wanted to reach out directly rather than assume anything. Specifically, I would be grateful for your permission to:

- cite and link to Sufinama as a source for the poems I work on;
- cross-reference your Bulleh Shah texts while I verify the original (public-domain) verses; and
- use the 76-item Bulleh Shah kaafi catalog in Urdu, diacritic Roman, and plain Roman as an aligned academic research corpus;
- preserve Sufinama's stable content, stanza, line, and token identifiers so every training example remains traceable to its source; and
- receive a machine-readable export, API access, or written authorization for a rate-limited research acquisition if an export is not available.

The intended use is a private, non-commercial academic research repository. Sufinama material would remain a distinct, fully attributed source-witness layer rather than being silently merged with other editions. Any public release, redistribution, or model release containing Sufinama-derived material would follow the terms of the collaboration we agree on.

I believe this genuinely furthers the mission Rekhta and Sufinama already champion. It brings this poetry to academic and international audiences with rigorous, respectful scholarship; it drives attribution and traffic back to Sufinama as the primary source; and it produces an openly licensed, citable resource that students and researchers can build on — all non-commercial, and all in the spirit of preservation and promotion that your foundation stands for.

I would be very happy to share the project repository, walk you through it on a call, and follow whatever attribution or usage guidelines you prefer. If there is a better person or process for a request like this, I would appreciate you pointing me in the right direction.

Thank you for the work you do to keep this tradition alive. I would be grateful for the chance to build on it responsibly and with your blessing.

With respect and gratitude,

Rauf Nawaz
